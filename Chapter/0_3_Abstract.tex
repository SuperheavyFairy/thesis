\begin{center}
	\Large{\textbf{ABSTRACT}}
\end{center}

The rapid development of implicit three-dimensional representations has significantly advanced automatic 3D reconstruction and generative modeling. Frameworks such as TRELLIS generate high-quality implicit geometry that is converted into polygonal meshes using differentiable isosurface extraction methods such as FlexiCubes. However, existing extraction algorithms mainly optimize geometric reconstruction accuracy while paying little attention to mesh topology. Consequently, the generated meshes often contain irregular edge flow and connectivity, limiting their suitability for modeling, subdivision, rigging, and animation without additional retopology.

This thesis proposes a topology-aware extension to the FlexiCubes framework to improve mesh structure while preserving geometric fidelity. High-quality meshes from the ShapeNetCore dataset are analyzed to derive local topology priors. A Transformer-based neural network is trained to predict surface normals and principal edge directions from sampled point clouds. During mesh extraction, the pretrained network provides topology supervision through additional loss functions that optimize the signed distance field, vertex deformation, and FlexiCubes weighting parameters. This encourages the extracted meshes to follow edge-flow patterns commonly found in artist-created models.

The proposed method is evaluated on multiple TRELLIS-generated models under different mesh simplification levels. Performance is assessed using four metrics: Root Mean Square (RMS) surface error, Hausdorff distance, surface normal deviation, and edge-direction error. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method preserves geometric reconstruction quality while consistently improving edge-flow preservation, producing meshes that are more suitable for practical 3D content creation and reducing the need for manual retopology.
